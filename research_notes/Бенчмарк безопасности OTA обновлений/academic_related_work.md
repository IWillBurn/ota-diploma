## Q1. Foundational software-update security papers (exact venues)

### Takeaway
The canonical lineage is: Bellissimo et al. (HotSec 2006), Cappos et al. "A Look in the Mirror" (CCS 2008), TUF (CCS 2010), Diplomat (NSDI 2016), Uptane (escar 2016, then IEEE VT Magazine 2018), Mercury (USENIX ATC 2017), CHAINIAC (2017), in-toto (USENIX Security 2019) and Sigstore (CCS 2022). Several venues in the brief need correcting. UpKit is ICDCS 2019, not ESORICS. The Bettayeb "survey" is an ArabWIC 2019 conference paper. Uptane's first paper appeared at escar in November 2016, i.e., escar Europe rather than escar USA (inference from the date). "CANDID" could not be found as an update-security system.

### Cited Findings (annotated bibliography)
- **Bellissimo, A., Burgess, J., Fu, K. (2006). "Secure Software Updates: Disappointments and New Challenges." USENIX HotSec 2006.** Analyses several popular deployed update mechanisms. Finds that they rely on trusted networks and are susceptible to weak man-in-the-middle attacks. Argues that embedded devices (intermittent connectivity, limited power) need new approaches. *Relevance:* the earliest "empirical study of updaters" and a historical baseline for the threat catalog. It also shows that cross-implementation evaluation of updaters is a 20-year-old research genre. — [OpenAlex W2122742966](https://openalex.org/W2122742966)
- **Cappos, J., Samuel, J., Baker, S., Hartman, J.H. (2008). "A Look in the Mirror: Attacks on Package Managers." ACM CCS 2008.** Studies the security of **ten** popular package managers and finds that all of them are exploitable by a man-in-the-middle or a malicious mirror. The authors obtained official mirrors under false credentials on all five distributions they tried, and estimate that a single mirror could compromise or crash hundreds to thousands of clients weekly. *Relevance:* **the most important precedent for the "threat × implementation matrix" contribution.** Its attack vocabulary (replay, freeze, endless data, slow retrieval, extraneous dependencies) seeds the thesis's malicious-server suite, and the thesis must explicitly position against it. — [DOI 10.1145/1455770.1455841](https://doi.org/10.1145/1455770.1455841)
- **Samuel, J., Mathewson, N., Cappos, J., Dingledine, R. (2010). "Survivable Key Compromise in Software Update Systems." ACM CCS 2010 (TUF).** Identifies three classes of information whose authenticity and integrity are critical for secure updates. Shows that existing update systems communicate them poorly under key compromise and have inadequate revocation. Designs TUF around principles for surviving key compromise. *Relevance:* the source of the role/threshold/expiry model and of the "online-key compromise" scenarios in the bench. — [DOI 10.1145/1866307.1866315](https://doi.org/10.1145/1866307.1866315)
- **Kuppusamy, T.K., Torres-Arias, S., Diaz, V., Cappos, J. (2016). "Diplomat: Using Delegations to Protect Community Repositories." USENIX NSDI 2016.** Introduces delegation mechanisms that give community repositories (PyPI, Docker Hub, RubyGems) compromise-resilience while still allowing real-time project registration. With Diplomat on PyPI, fewer than 1% of users are at risk even if an attacker controls the repository undetected for a month. *Relevance:* the source of delegation and terminating-delegation semantics. These are a prime target for differential testing, because implementations disagree on delegation traversal; tuf-conformance tracking lists a go-tuf delegation-tracing issue, see Q4. — [OpenAlex W2309925045](https://openalex.org/W2309925045)
- **Kuppusamy, T.K., Diaz, V., Cappos, J. (2017). "Mercury: Bandwidth-Effective Prevention of Rollback Attacks Against Community Repositories." USENIX ATC 2017.** Proposes a bandwidth-efficient way to prevent rollback attacks on community repositories; it later fed into TUF's snapshot design. *Relevance:* the rollback/freeze scenario family. — [OpenAlex W2740863337](https://openalex.org/W2740863337)
- **Kuppusamy, T.K., Brown, A., Awwad, S., McCoy, D., Bielawski, R., Mott, C., Lauzon, S., Weimerskirch, A., Cappos, J. (2016). "Uptane: Securing Software Updates for Automobiles." escar 2016 (November 2016).** The first academic presentation of Uptane, which adapts TUF to vehicles (Image/Director repositories, primary/secondary ECUs, full and partial verification). *Relevance:* the baseline threat model for the automotive part of the catalog. — [PDF, NYU SSL](https://ssl.engineering.nyu.edu/papers/kuppusamy_escar_16.pdf); citation details (author list, Nov 2016) from [Awwad timeline](https://awwad.github.io/timeline.html) and [Wikiwand Uptane draft](https://extension.wikiwand.com/en/Draft:Uptane). The November date implies escar Europe rather than escar USA; this is an inference, not stated explicitly by the sources.
- **Kuppusamy, T.K., DeLong, L.A., Cappos, J. (2018). "Uptane: Security and Customizability of Software Updates for Vehicles." IEEE Vehicular Technology Magazine 13(1).** Journal-level description of Uptane's security and customizability. — [DOI 10.1109/MVT.2017.2778751](https://doi.org/10.1109/mvt.2017.2778751)
- **Uptane in USENIX ;login:** — **UNVERIFIED.** An article in ;login: (around 2017) is commonly cited, but no record was retrieved in this session.
- **Nikitin, K., Kokoris-Kogias, E., Jovanovic, P., Gailly, N., Gasser, L., Khoffi, I., Cappos, J., Ford, B. (2017). "CHAINIAC: Proactive Software-Update Transparency via Collectively Signed Skipchains and Verified Builds."** A decentralized update framework: witness servers collectively verify releases, build verifiers check source-to-binary correspondence, and a collectively signed skipchain lets arbitrarily stale clients catch up. Evaluated on reproducible Debian packages and PyPI data. *Relevance:* transparency/freshness design point and the "stale client catch-up" scenario. — [OpenAlex W2729024603](https://openalex.org/W2729024603). OpenAlex points to the EPFL Infoscience copy, so **the USENIX Security 2017 venue is as commonly cited but was not re-confirmed on usenix.org in this session.**
- **Torres-Arias, S., Afzali, H., Kuppusamy, T.K., Curtmola, R., Cappos, J. (2019). "in-toto: Providing Farm-to-Table Guarantees for Bits and Bytes." USENIX Security 2019.** A supply-chain layout/attestation framework. *Relevance:* background; out of scope for client-side OTA testing except where Uptane+in-toto are combined (see Scudo, Q7). — [OpenAlex W2967772953](https://openalex.org/W2967772953)
- **Newman, Z., Meyers, J.S., Torres-Arias, S. (2022). "Sigstore: Software Signing for Everybody." ACM CCS 2022.** Identity-based keyless signing with transparency logs. *Relevance:* Sigstore's client trust root is distributed via TUF, and the Sigstore community maintains a parallel conformance suite (Q4). This is a model for an "executable conformance + daily report" culture. — [DOI 10.1145/3548606.3560596](https://doi.org/10.1145/3548606.3560596)
- **Al-Bassam, M., Meiklejohn, S. (2018). "Contour: A Practical System for Binary Transparency." LNCS (DPM/CBT 2018 workshop volume).** Binary transparency anchored in a blockchain. *Relevance:* background for "targeted/split-view update" threats. — [DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-00305-0_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-00305-0_8). That this DOI volume is the ESORICS-2018-satellite DPM/CBT proceedings is my inference from the ISBN and was not checked.
- **Abdullah, L., Hahn, S., Freiling, F. (2019). "Achieving Consistency of Software Updates against Strong Attackers." EICC 2019.** Defines requirements against targeted malicious updates (a supplier coerced or compromised into sending handcrafted updates to a few users) and implements an APT extension. *Relevance:* the "targeted update / equivocation" threat class; few test suites cover it. — [DOI 10.1145/3360664.3360670](https://doi.org/10.1145/3360664.3360670)
- **Magnanini, F., Ferretti, L., Colajanni, M. (2021). "Scalable, Confidential and Survivable Software Updates." IEEE TPDS.** A survivable plus confidential update framework using multi-authority ABE. *Relevance:* background (confidential updates, which SUIT also supports via encrypted payloads). — [DOI 10.1109/TPDS.2021.3090330](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpds.2021.3090330)
- **"CANDID"** — **UNVERIFIED / not found.** No update-security system called CANDID was found in OpenAlex or web searches. Recommend dropping it unless the student has a specific reference.

### Inferences
- The thesis's threat vocabulary (freeze, replay/rollback, endless data, slow retrieval, mix-and-match, key compromise) is already canonical (CCS 2008, CCS 2010). The catalog's novelty therefore cannot be "new threats". It has to lie in **unification across specs, machine-readability, traceability to regulation, and executability**.
- "A Look in the Mirror" is effectively a 2008 threat × implementation study over 10 package managers. The thesis must cite it as the methodological ancestor and argue its difference: cross-spec (TUF, Uptane, SUIT, A/B), executable and reproducible, and including fault-tolerance.

### Gaps
- The exact USENIX page for CHAINIAC and the ;login: Uptane article were not fetched.
- The Uptane escar PDF was found via search but not opened.

## Q2. Formal verification of update protocols (TUF / Uptane / SUIT and others)

### Takeaway
Uptane has received substantial formal analysis since 2022: CSP/FDR-based test generation (2022), Tamarin (Boureanu 2023), and Kind 2 + Tamarin (Lorch et al., RAID 2024, which found 6 new flaws). Other automotive and IoT update designs (UniSUF, UpKit, a UN R156 SUMS) have ProVerif, Tamarin or Event-B models. **No dedicated Tamarin/ProVerif analysis of TUF itself or of the SUIT manifest/RFC 9019 architecture was found.** The only SUIT-related formal work found is Tacchella et al. (ACM TCPS), and it extends SUIT rather than verifying it. Formal work checks *designs*; none of it measures *implementations*. That separation is the thesis's opening.

### Cited Findings (annotated bibliography)
- **Lorch, R., Larraz, D., Tinelli, C., Chowdhury, O. (2024). "A Comprehensive, Automated Security Analysis of the Uptane Automotive Over-the-Air Update Framework." RAID 2024.** Eagerly combines an infinite-state model checker (Kind 2) with a cryptographic protocol verifier (Tamarin). It uses state-space reductions and a meta-level analysis over Uptane's optional features. It rediscovered all 5 previously known vulnerabilities and found 6 new ones, all acknowledged by the Uptane standards body. *Relevance:* **closest formal work.** Its vulnerabilities should become concrete test cases in the bench, which would test whether implementations (e.g., aktualizr) exhibit them. — [DOI 10.1145/3678890.3678927](https://doi.org/10.1145/3678890.3678927); [author PDF](https://lorchrob.github.io/papers/uptane.pdf); [RAID 2024 PDF](https://raid2024.github.io/papers/raid2024-37.pdf); finding counts per [search summary of the paper](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/a-comprehensive-automated-security-analysis-of-the-uptane-automot/)
- **Boureanu, I. (2023). "Formally Verifying the Security and Privacy of an Adopted Standard for Software-Update in Cars: Verifying Uptane 2.0." IEEE SMC 2023.** Tamarin analysis of Uptane 2.0 using the standard's own threat model plus extended attacker tiers, followed by responsible disclosure to the Uptane Alliance. *Relevance:* a second, independent formal source of Uptane threats for the catalog. — [DOI 10.1109/SMC53992.2023.10394216](https://doi.org/10.1109/smc53992.2023.10394216)
- **Kirk, R., Nguyen, H.N., Bryans, J.W., Shaikh, S.A., Wartnaby, C.E. (2022). "A Formal Framework for Security Testing of Automotive Over-the-Air Update Systems." Journal of Logical and Algebraic Methods in Programming (JLAMP).** Translates Uptane and an attack model into CSP, verifies them to generate an exhaustive list of test cases, then runs the tests on a testbed with an Uptane implementation. *Relevance:* **close prior work** that bridges formal models and executable tests, but for a single spec and single implementation. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jlamp.2022.100812](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlamp.2022.100812)
- **Ponsard, C., Darquennes, D. (2021). "Towards Formal Security Verification of Over-the-Air Update Protocol: Requirements, Survey and UpKit Case Study." ICISSP 2021.** Surveys update-protocol requirements (including Uptane) and models UpKit in Tamarin. *Relevance:* a requirements list that can be cross-checked against the thesis catalog. — [DOI 10.5220/0010431408000808](https://doi.org/10.5220/0010431408000808); Tamarin/UpKit detail per [SciTePress page](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/104314)
- **Hagen, M.S., Lundqvist, E., Phu, A., Wang, Y., Strandberg, K., Schiller, E.M. (2025). "Towards a Formal Verification of Secure Vehicle Software Updates." Computers & Security, 104751.** A ProVerif framework for UniSUF that checks confidentiality, integrity, authenticity, freshness, order and liveness. *Relevance:* the property list (notably "order" and "liveness") is useful vocabulary for the benchmark's metrics. — [DOI 10.1016/j.cose.2025.104751](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2025.104751); [arXiv 2511.15479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15479)
- **Strandberg, K., Oka, D.K., Olovsson, T. (2021). "UniSUF: a unified software update framework for vehicles utilizing isolation techniques and trusted execution environments." escar Europe 2021.** — [DOI 10.13154/294-8353](https://doi.org/10.13154/294-8353)
- **Seo, J., Kwak, J., Kim, S. (2023). "Formally Verified Software Update Management System in Automotive." VehicleSec 2023 (NDSS).** Threat modelling to refine the abstract UN R156 requirements, followed by an Event-B-verified SUMS architecture. *Relevance:* **a precedent for mapping regulation (UN R156) to concrete security requirements.** This is the same move the thesis makes for CRA and FSTEK, done with Event-B instead of tests. — [DOI 10.14722/vehiclesec.2023.23087](https://doi.org/10.14722/vehiclesec.2023.23087)
- **Mazhar, S., Rakib, A., Doss, R., Anwar, A., Jiang, F. (2025). "Integrating Threat Analysis and Formal Verification for Secure OTA Updates." IEEE PRDC 2025.** ThreatGet threat modelling turned into CTL properties, checked in NuSMV on a single shared system model. *Relevance:* a "threat catalog to verifiable property" pipeline. The thesis does "threat catalog to executable test". — [DOI 10.1109/PRDC67299.2025.00013](https://doi.org/10.1109/prdc67299.2025.00013)
- **Tacchella, A., Beozzo, E., Crispo, B., Roveri, M. (2025/2026). "Firmware Secure Updates Meet Formal Verification." ACM Transactions on Cyber-Physical Systems.** Extends IETF SUIT with SBOM integration and a "Behavioral Certification Manifest" that gives formal guarantees about update content; prototype evaluated on real-world benchmarks. *Relevance:* the only recent SUIT-centric academic work with "formal" in scope found. It verifies update *content*, not manifest-processor robustness. — [DOI 10.1145/3754455](https://doi.org/10.1145/3754455); [IRIS UniTN record](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/463775). The year conflicts: OpenAlex says 2025 and the search snippet says 2026 (likely online-first versus issue).
- **IETF SUIT behavioural-manifest draft (Moran, 2019, expired).** Proposes a formally specifiable description of update/secure-boot behaviour. The SUIT command language is linear (no branches or loops), with conditions that have no side effects and directives that do. *Relevance:* this structure makes SUIT processors amenable to differential testing and grammar-based fuzzing. — [draft-moran-suit-behavioural-manifest-00](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-moran-suit-behavioural-manifest-00); current manifest spec [draft-ietf-suit-manifest-30 (Dec 2024)](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-suit-manifest-30.html)
- **Tudorache, L., Kurtev, I., van den Brand, M. (2026). "Bridging Theory and Practice: An Executable Taxonomy of Security Properties for ProVerif and Tamarin." arXiv 2605.29465.** A taxonomy of security properties drawn from 53 studies (2022–2025), with executable models. It does not cover update protocols. *Relevance:* tangential, but uses the "executable taxonomy" framing similar to the thesis's "executable catalog". This framing is already in use, so name the contribution carefully. — [arXiv 2605.29465](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29465v1)

### Inferences
- Formal analyses (Lorch 2024, Boureanu 2023) give design-level ground truth. A strong thesis move is a **"formal-findings to executable test" bridge**: encode each acknowledged Uptane design flaw as a bench scenario and report which implementations are affected. The cited formal papers did not do this. Kirk et al. 2022 did it for one implementation using CSP-generated tests.
- TUF itself and SUIT lack published symbolic analyses. This is not the thesis's job, but it can be listed as future work. It also means the bench's threat catalog for SUIT must be derived from RFC 9019 / RFC 9124 threat models rather than from formal results.

### Gaps
- No Tamarin/ProVerif model of TUF (spec 1.0.x) or of the SUIT manifest/RFC 9019 was found. The absence of evidence is not conclusive, because dblp could not be queried.
- The full list of the 6 new Lorch et al. findings was not extracted. Read the paper before building test cases.
- The formal tool used by Tacchella et al. was not identified (full text was not accessible, HTTP 403).

## Q3. IoT, embedded and automotive update frameworks, surveys, and supply-chain SoKs

### Takeaway
There are many surveys and design frameworks (ASSURED, UpKit, the SUIT-on-RIOT reality check, the El Jaouhari & Bouvet survey, the Halder et al. survey, Catuogno & Galdi, and the DeOTA-IoT catalog), plus supply-chain SoKs (Ladisa S&P 2023, Okafor SCORED 2022, the AStRA SoK 2024). None of them provides an executable, cross-implementation evaluation. The surveys are taxonomies, and the frameworks evaluate their own prototype. DeOTA-IoT (2025) is the closest to a "catalog". It catalogs *design techniques* mapped to quality attributes, not threats mapped to tests.

## Q4. Empirical and implementation-level studies of update systems; real-world attacks

### Takeaway
Implementation-level evidence exists, but it is fragmented:
- IoT companion-app update SDKs (AoT, EuroS&P 2023).
- Android OS update logic (Pileup, S&P 2014) and bootloaders (BootStomp 2017; NDSS 2025 memory-safety study).
- Uptane security testing on a single implementation (Mahmood et al. 2020/2022; Kirk et al. 2022).
- Industry exploit talks: Keen Lab Tesla 2018 and Windows Downdate 2024.
- Community conformance suites: **tuf-conformance**, the older **tuf-test-vectors**, and **sigstore-conformance**.

None of these spans multiple update ecosystems.

### Cited Findings (annotated bibliography)

## Q5. Testing-methodology analogues to borrow (conformance, differential, fuzzing benchmarks, crash consistency)

### Takeaway
TLS gives the closest methodological templates:
- TLS-Attacker (CCS 2016): a configurable attacker framework.
- TLS-Anvil (USENIX Security 2022): RFC-derived combinatorial tests over 13 libraries, with categorized outcomes.
- Protocol-state fuzzing of TLS and DTLS.

Others are:
- Certificate-validation differential testing: Frankencerts (S&P 2014), mucert (FSE 2015), NEZHA (S&P 2017).
- Differential testing of 22 JSON parsers (AsiaCCS 2024) and of 43 JWT implementations (JWTeemo, NDSS 2026).
- The QUIC Interop Runner (containerized adapters, about 10 implementations).
- Fuzzing-evaluation methodology: Klees CCS 2018, Magma, FuzzBench, and the Schloegel SoK at S&P 2024.
- Crash-consistency and power-fault injection: ALICE and "Torturing Databases" (OSDI 2014), CrashMonkey (OSDI 2018).

### Inferences
Metrics and design elements worth copying:
1. **Outcome taxonomy rather than binary pass/fail** (as in TLS-Anvil): e.g., *secure-reject*, *secure-but-non-conformant*, *unsafe-accept*, *crash/hang*, *bricked*, *recovered-to-previous*, *N/A*.
2. **Ground-truth validation** (Magma): re-introduce known CVEs and fixed bugs in pinned versions of clients to prove that each test detects what it claims.
3. **Statistical rigor for fuzzing** (Klees, Schloegel).
4. **Crash-state enumeration** (ALICE/CrashMonkey) rather than random power cuts. This lets the thesis report "coverage of crash points" (e.g., the percentage of write/fsync boundaries tested), a metric the practitioner reports lack.
5. **Containerized adapters plus a network simulator** (QUIC Interop Runner) for slow, endless and frozen data.
6. **Differential consensus oracle** (Möller 2024) for TUF/Uptane metadata parsing and SUIT manifest processing.

- No academic paper was found that applies ALICE/CrashMonkey-style crash-state enumeration to OTA updaters or bootloaders. This is a strong novelty point, but the absence should be stated as "to the best of our knowledge".
- The TLS-Anvil outcome-category names were not re-read in this session (the taxonomy above is a proposal, not a quotation).

## Q8. Closest prior work (top 8), novelty and gap analysis, and defensible phrasing

### Takeaway
No existing work, academic or community, was found that offers an **executable benchmark spanning TUF, Uptane, SUIT and A/B image updaters** under one adapter protocol, one machine-readable threat catalog and one result matrix. No work was found that combines **adversarial-repository tests with power-loss crash-state testing** for update clients. Each ingredient has strong prior art, though. The contribution is defensible as **integration plus new dimensions (cross-ecosystem, fault tolerance, regulatory traceability), validated empirically on 9–10 implementations with upstreamed fixes and tests.** It should not be framed as new attacks or a new test technique.

### Cited Findings: closest prior work, ranked by similarity
1. **tuf-conformance (TUF project, 2024–2026).** An executable, multi-client, adapter-based (CLI `init`/`refresh`/`download`) conformance suite for TUF clients with expected-failure files. *Overlap:* contributions 2 (adapter) and 5 (upstreaming) for TUF. *Difference:* TUF only, conformance rather than differential, and no Uptane, SUIT or A/B. Power-loss and resource-exhaustion (endless/slow data) coverage was not confirmed. — [GitHub](https://github.com/theupdateframework/tuf-conformance); [CLIENT-CLI.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/theupdateframework/tuf-conformance/main/CLIENT-CLI.md)
2. **tuf-test-vectors (heartsucker / uptane).** Cross-spec (TUF + Uptane) test vectors with an interactive server. *Overlap:* cross-spec executable vectors. *Difference:* legacy, no SUIT or A/B, no fault injection, no regulatory mapping. — [GitHub](https://github.com/heartsucker/tuf-test-vectors)
3. **Mahmood, Nguyen, Shaikh (Vehicular Communications 2022) with Mahmood et al. 4. **Kirk et al. (JLAMP 2022).** CSP models of Uptane plus an attacker generate exhaustive test cases executed on a testbed. *Overlap:* systematic test derivation. *Difference:* one spec and one implementation. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlamp.2022.100812)

6. **Ibrahim, Continella, Bianchi, "AoT" (EuroS&P 2023).** A threat model, an issue categorization and measurement of 23 devices and 6 SDKs, scaled to 1,356 apps. *Overlap:* threat taxonomy plus multi-implementation empirical results. *Difference:* app-mediated IoT, no standard frameworks, no executable reusable bench. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/eurosp57164.2023.00065)

|---|---|---|---|
| (1) Machine-readable threat catalog mapped to TUF/Uptane/SUIT + CRA + FSTEK, with test-coverage analysis of existing suites | Surveys (El Jaouhari 2022, Halder 2020); attack trees (Mahmood 2022; Ladisa 2023); DeOTA-IoT technique catalog (2025); regulation-to-model mapping (Seo 2023 for UN R156) | **Medium-high** | Cross-spec unification with stable IDs. **Coverage analysis of existing suites** (what tuf-conformance, tuf-test-vectors and vendor tests cover) is a measurable, novel result. **Risks to the novelty claim**
1. tuf-conformance already covers much of the TUF column. Quantify the delta: tests added, threat IDs newly covered, bugs found that the suite missed.
2. "A Look in the Mirror" and AoT already produced threat × implementation matrices. Avoid "first matrix".
3. Mahmood/Kirk already did threat-model-driven executable testing of Uptane. Avoid "first systematic security testing of OTA".
4. Uptane's POUF design means implementations may not be wire-compatible ([Moore et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.4271/11-02-02-0011)). Comparability across Uptane implementations is a threat to validity.
5. Heterogeneous threat models (TUF repository compromise versus SUIT constrained-device versus A/B power loss) can make one matrix look apples-to-oranges. Define per-cell applicability and outcome semantics up front.
6. Regulatory mappings (CRA Annex I is principle-based) are interpretive. Present them as traceability, not as compliance certification.
7. Fuzzing claims face the reviewer expectations set by Schloegel et al. 2024.
8. Absence-of-prior-art statements rest on OpenAlex and web search. dblp and eLibrary could not be queried, so do a final check before submission.

**Suggested defensible phrasing (EN / RU)**
- EN: "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first open, executable benchmark that evaluates update clients from *different* update-security ecosystems (TUF, Uptane, IETF SUIT, and A/B image updaters) against a single machine-readable threat catalog, traceable both to the specifications' threat models and to regulatory requirements (EU CRA, FSTEK), and that measures both adversarial-repository security and power-loss fault tolerance within one harness. We build on and extend the TUF conformance suite, add differential and crash-consistency oracles, and report results for N implementations, of which K tests/fixes were accepted upstream."
- RU: «Насколько нам известно, впервые предложен открытый исполняемый бенчмарк, позволяющий единообразно оценивать клиенты обновления разных экосистем (TUF, Uptane, IETF SUIT, A/B-обновляторы) по единому машиночитаемому каталогу угроз, трассируемому к моделям угроз спецификаций и к требованиям CRA и ФСТЭК России, с одновременной оценкой защищённости от атак со стороны репозитория/сети и устойчивости к сбоям питания».
- Avoid the words "first threat taxonomy", "first conformance suite", "first OTA security testing", and "proves compliance".

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether tuf-conformance already contains slow-retrieval, endless-data or key-rotation-under-compromise tests. This needs a code-level check (`tuf_conformance/` test files) before writing the delta.
- Not verified: whether OSS-Fuzz already fuzzes the targeted parsers (e.g., MCUboot, libcsuit, tough). Check before claiming fuzzing novelty.
